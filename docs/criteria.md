## Industry

- **Target sectors**: B2B services with recurring contracts, niche distribution (specialty wholesale, industrial supply), light manufacturing with durable demand, professional services with sticky clients (accounting/IT-managed-services/HR consulting), home services with route density.
- **Open to with reservations**: specialty retail with strong unit economics and limited online competition; e-commerce only if it owns its brand and isn't dependent on a single platform.
- **Hard nos**: restaurants and food service, gas stations, convenience stores, tobacco/cannabis/firearms, single-location retail without recurring revenue, multi-level marketing, anything illegal at federal level, businesses whose primary moat is the current owner's personal relationships.

## Geography

- **Primary**: South Florida — Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach counties.
- **Secondary**: Florida statewide (Tampa, Orlando, Jacksonville metros), Southeast US (Georgia, North/South Carolina, Tennessee).
- **Hard nos**: California, New York / Northeast, anywhere requiring relocation, anywhere with a state income tax he isn't willing to take on.

## Financial profile

- **Asking price range**: `$500,000` – `$3,000,000`
- **SDE range**: `$200,000` – `$800,000`
- **Revenue range**: `$1,000,000` – `$8,000,000` (rough 3–10× SDE band)
- **Multiple ceiling**: `3.5×` SDE — willing to stretch to 4× for businesses with >50% recurring revenue and a working management team.
- **Acceptable financing structures**:
  - SBA 7(a): yes
  - Seller financing (% of price): minimum 10%, prefer 15–20%+
  - Equity needed: approximately `$150,000` – `$300,000` personal cash-down

## Operating profile

- **Employee count**: `5` – `30` FTE — large enough to have a working management layer, small enough that one owner-operator change isn't catastrophic.
- **Owner involvement post-close**: **active operator** — Mr. Alonso plans to run the business day-to-day in year one and transition toward strategic oversight in years 2–3.
- **Years established (minimum)**: `7+` years — filters out fads, unproven models, and businesses that haven't survived a downturn.
- **Recurring revenue %** (minimum): `30%` — protects valuation and forecastability; ideal target is 50%+.
- **Owner-dependency tolerance**: **low** — avoid businesses where the owner is the brand, the only salesperson, or the only person who can do the technical work.

## Growth & risk

- **Customer concentration ceiling**: largest customer ≤ `20%` of revenue.
- **Trailing performance**: 3-year revenue stable or growing. Single down year acceptable if explained (one-time event, intentional pruning); declining 3-year trend is an automatic flag.
- **Cyclicality tolerance**: **low to moderate** — avoid pure construction, discretionary consumer spending, commodity-exposed businesses. Comfortable with mild B2B-services seasonality.
- **Regulatory risk tolerance**: **low** — avoid healthcare, finance, child-care, anything where a single regulatory or licensing change is existential.

## Hard knockouts (instant pass — no Run Analysis needed)

These are bright lines. If a listing hits any of these, kill it.

- Declining revenue 3 years running.
- Single customer over 30% of revenue.
- Owner is the only person who can do the technical work, and there's no realistic 12-month training path.
- Requires a license Mr. Alonso doesn't have and can't acquire within 12 months (medical, legal, certain trades, regulated transport).
- Business model dependent on a single third-party platform (Amazon FBA single-product, Google AdSense as primary revenue, single Shopify store with no brand of its own).
- Pending litigation, IRS lien, or unresolved environmental issue.
- Asking price > 4× SDE without a clear, quantified reason.

## Soft preferences (positive signals, not requirements)

- Recurring revenue model (subscription, service contracts, route density).
- Diversified customer base (top-5 ≤ 35% of revenue).
- Owner willing to stay on for 12-month transition with structured handoff.
- Real estate available to lease at market (not bundled into the sale at a premium).
- Documented systems, SOPs, and at least one person below the owner who could run the day-to-day.
- B2B over B2C when otherwise equivalent.
- Existing CRM / ERP / accounting system in use (Hubspot, NetSuite, QuickBooks Online — anything other than paper or spreadsheets).

## Notes for the Analyst agent

When scoring a listing against this document:

- Treat **Hard nos** and **Hard knockouts** as binary fails. Flag the listing prominently and recommend "pass" as next action.
- Treat **Targets**, **Primary geography**, and the **Operating profile** numerics as scoring weights — closer to target = higher score.
- Treat **Soft preferences** as positive modifiers but not deal-makers on their own.
- When a listing doesn't disclose the data needed to evaluate a criterion, mark it "not disclosed" in the fit assessment — never extrapolate.
- Be aware these are V1 placeholders (see banner at top). If a listing fits *every* criterion suspiciously well, the issue is more likely that the criteria are too generic than that the listing is exceptional — flag it for the user to refine criteria.
